Two faults the ruler found

Neither screen could be seen without a Mac, so both were rebuilt in the browser at 1 pt = 1 px and measured in the DOM. Two problems came out that reading the code had not surfaced.

Half the live screen was empty

Gap above speed213.5 pt → 129 pt
Gap below speed213.5 pt → 208 pt
Readout size92 pt → 148 pt

Two equal spacers put 427 pt of dead space around a 92 pt number on an 852 pt screen — the readout floated, unanchored, earning none of its clearance. It is now large enough to occupy the space and sits above the optical centre, with peak speed beneath it and the lower half given to progress and telemetry.

The distance marks had no uncertainty

Name column83 pt → flexible
Fixed columns266 pt → 0 pt
± shownno → yes

As a four-column table the marks fit a 393 pt phone only by squeezing the name column to 83 pt and dropping the ± entirely — the one figure the spec insists must never be dropped, on the row a drag racer actually cares about. Each mark is now two lines: the rollout ET with its uncertainty promoted, from-rest and trap demoted beneath. Nothing was lost, and it reads at a glance instead of by column scan.

Rules the screens follow

Why it looks like this

  • The staging tree is structural, not ornamental. Each bulb is one of the three conditions the app requires before it will arm — satellite lock, solved vehicle frame, detected stillness. The unlit bulb answers why won't it arm, which is the only question anyone asks in a car park. The green bulb is the launch.
  • The ± is set co-equal with the number. A 0–60 of 4.31 ± 0.04 s is an honest number; 4.31 s alone is not. Rendering the uncertainty small and grey would quietly undo the entire reason the app post-processes at all.
  • Digits are monospaced everywhere. A timeslip is machine-printed, and a proportional digit set makes a speed readout jitter sideways as it counts.
  • The live screen shows no elapsed time. Results come only from the backward smoothing pass after the run ends, so a running clock would invite trust in a number that is not the answer. The time appears once, on the timeslip.
  • Amber, green and red mean what they mean on a strip. They are staging-tree vocabulary, used only for the states those lights stand for — never as decoration.
Screens depict a fixed-appearance app and do not follow this page's light or dark theme. Numbers are from a synthetic run with a known answer, not a measured car.
